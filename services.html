Therapy at HHH

Therapy that makes sense in real life

CBT isn't about being told to "think positively," and it isn't about pretending difficult things don't feel difficult. It's about understanding what's happening, noticing the patterns that might be keeping you stuck, and finding practical ways to respond differently.

Sound familiar?

Maybe this feels familiar

Your mind replays the same conversation on a loop, long after it's actually over.

You're tired, but your brain picks bedtime as the moment to run through everything you're worried about.

You set yourself a standard you'd never expect from anyone else, then feel like you've failed when you can't keep up with it.

You've started saying no to things, just to avoid how anxious they make you feel.

Or maybe nothing feels obviously wrong. You just know: "I don't want to keep feeling like this."
The service

1:1 Cognitive Behavioural Therapy

Let's figure out what's keeping things stuck.

CBT is built on a simple idea: what we think shapes how we feel, and how we feel shapes what we do, which then reinforces what we think. Working through that loop together, with structure and a shared plan, is what one-to-one CBT with me looks like.

How we work

The HHH CBT loop

Not a one-off conversation. A repeating loop we work through together, session by session.

01

Notice

What's happening? Thoughts, emotions, behaviours, physical sensations and the situation around them.

02

Understand

We connect the dots and build a shared understanding of what's keeping the difficulty going.

03

Experiment

This is where therapy leaves the room. We test things, practise skills, and try different responses in real life.

04

Reflect

What changed? What did you learn? What do we keep, adapt, or try next?

↻ And we keep learning from there.

What we treat

Support for what's actually going on

Every therapy plan is tailored, but these are some of the areas where CBT can be helpful.

Anxiety & worry

Your mind won't switch off.

Racing thoughts and constant "what ifs" can leave you stuck, even avoiding things just to escape them. CBT helps you interrupt the cycle and respond differently.

Depression & low mood

Everything feels harder than it used to.

Low energy and pulling away from things you enjoy tend to build on each other. CBT helps you rebuild momentum, one small step at a time.

Stress & burnout

You're doing a lot, but it never feels like enough.

Constantly switched on with no real recovery makes it hard to know where to start. We'll find what's driving it, then build habits that actually hold.

Trauma

Something happened, and it still feels present.

Difficult experiences can keep shaping how you think and react long after they're over. Therapy creates space to process it at your own pace.

OCD

The thought keeps coming back, and you keep responding to make it go away.

Checking, reassurance-seeking and compulsions can take over more of your day than you'd like. We'll work on breaking that cycle together.

Self-esteem

That inner critic has a lot to say.

Constant comparison and never feeling "enough" often trace back to old beliefs. CBT helps you test those beliefs against the evidence.

In a session

What does CBT actually look like?

1

Making sense of a difficult situation

Breaking something down together and looking at what actually happened.

2

Spotting patterns

Understanding the connections between thoughts, feelings, behaviours and physical responses.

3

Trying something differently

Behavioural experiments, exposure, activity scheduling, problem-solving, or other CBT strategies where appropriate.

4

Practising between sessions

Small experiments outside therapy, so the work connects to actual life.

5

Reviewing what's changing

Looking together at what's helping, what isn't, and where we go next.

CBT is collaborative. I'm bringing psychological knowledge and a framework; you're bringing expertise in your own life. We need both.
